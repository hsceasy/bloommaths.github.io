The Locked Laboratory — Standard Edition

Seven rooms of integer operations, ordering, sign conventions, BEDMAS and bracket placement.

📋 Classroom setup

Students click objects in each laboratory room, solve the integer puzzles, and unlock the exit. Optional hints, worked examples and mini-lessons are built into the student game. Encourage students to record their calculations and explain their sign choices.

The final room uses an interactive bracket-placement puzzle: the answer key shows the intended bracketed expression, rather than the game’s internal click-position code. The student game does not send results to this dashboard.

ROOM 01 · 3 PUZZLES

The Temperature Chamber

The laboratory door is sealed. Restore the thermometer, computer and keypad systems by solving their integer puzzles.

01.1🌡️ Temperature System
The laboratory temperature is −4°C. The heating system raises it by 9°C. What is the new temperature in °C?
Answer: 5
Hint

Start at −4 on a number line. A rise of 9 means move nine spaces to the right.

Worked example

Worked example: A freezer is at −6°C and warms by 8°C. Calculate −6 + 8 = 2, so the new temperature is 2°C.

Mini-lesson

Mini-lesson: Adding a positive integer means moving right on a number line. For example, −3 + 5 = 2. Begin at −3, count five steps to the right: −2, −1, 0, 1, 2.

01.2💻 Computer System
The computer records a temperature of 3°C. Overnight, the temperature falls by 8°C. What is the new temperature in °C?
Answer: -5
Hint

A fall of 8 means subtract 8. Start at 3 and move eight spaces to the left.

Worked example

Worked example: The temperature is 4°C and falls by 7°C. Calculate 4 − 7 = −3, so it becomes −3°C.

Mini-lesson

Mini-lesson: Subtracting a positive integer means moving left on a number line. For example, 2 − 5 = −3. Start at 2 and move five steps left: 1, 0, −1, −2, −3.

01.3🔢 Security Keypad
The keypad shows three readings: −7, 2 and −3. What is their sum?
Answer: -8
Hint

Add the two negative readings first, then add 2.

Worked example

Worked example: −4 + 3 + (−2) = −1 + (−2) = −3.

Mini-lesson

Mini-lesson: You can add integers in any order. Combine negative numbers together: −7 + (−3) = −10. Then add the positive number: −10 + 2 = −8.

ROOM 02 · 3 PUZZLES

The Order of Operations Lab

The next door has a three-part combination lock. Solve three BEDMAS puzzles involving negative numbers. Each answer is positive. Enter the answers into the lock in order: A, B, C.

02.1🧪 Puzzle A — Chemical Mixer
Evaluate −3 + 7 × 2
Answer: 11
Hint

Multiply 7 × 2 before adding the negative 3.

Worked example

Worked example: −5 + 4 × 3 = −5 + 12 = 7.

Mini-lesson

BEDMAS means Brackets, Exponents, Division and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction. Division and multiplication have equal priority (work left to right), as do addition and subtraction. Here, first calculate 7 × 2 = 14, then −3 + 14 = 11.

02.2⚙️ Puzzle B — Power Regulator
Evaluate (−18 + 30) ÷ 3
Answer: 4
Hint

Work out the sum inside the brackets before dividing.

Worked example

Worked example: (−8 + 18) ÷ 2 = 10 ÷ 2 = 5.

Mini-lesson

Brackets come first. Calculate −18 + 30 = 12, then divide by 3 to get 4. Adding a positive number to a negative number can cross zero.

02.3📡 Puzzle C — Signal Console
Evaluate 20 ÷ (−5) + 7 × 2
Answer: 10
Hint

Divide by the negative number and multiply before you add. A positive divided by a negative is negative.

Worked example

Worked example: 12 ÷ (−3) + 3 × 5 = −4 + 15 = 11.

Mini-lesson

Division and multiplication have equal priority, and both come before addition. First 20 ÷ (−5) = −4 and 7 × 2 = 14. Then −4 + 14 = 10. Remember: a positive number divided by a negative number gives a negative answer.

ROOM 03 · 4 PUZZLES

The Comparison Observatory

Four observation stations compare integers. Type < or > for each comparison, then enter the four symbols in order into the special symbol lock to open the next door.

03.1🔭 Station A — Deep Freeze
Compare −8 ___ −3. Type < or >.
Answer: <
Hint

On a number line, the number further left is smaller.

Worked example

Worked example: −9 < −2 because −9 is further left on a number line.

Mini-lesson

Negative numbers get smaller as you move further below zero. −8 is further left than −3, so −8 < −3.

03.2🪐 Station B — Orbit Monitor
Compare 4 ___ −6. Type < or >.
Answer: >
Hint

Any positive integer is greater than any negative integer.

Worked example

Worked example: 2 > −7 because positive numbers are greater than negative numbers.

Mini-lesson

Positive integers sit to the right of zero; negative integers sit to the left. So 4 > −6.

03.3🧭 Station C — Pressure Gauge
Compare −2 ___ −11. Type < or >.
Answer: >
Hint

Which number is closer to zero on the negative side?

Worked example

Worked example: −3 > −10 because −3 is to the right of −10.

Mini-lesson

With two negative integers, the number closer to zero is greater. −2 is greater than −11, so −2 > −11.

03.4🌙 Station D — Lunar Scanner
Compare −5 ___ 0. Type < or >.
Answer: <
Hint

Every negative integer is less than zero.

Worked example

Worked example: −1 < 0 because −1 is left of zero.

Mini-lesson

Zero is greater than all negative integers and less than all positive integers. Therefore −5 < 0.

ROOM 04 · 4 PUZZLES

The Power Generator

Restart four generator circuits by multiplying and dividing positive and negative integers. Enter the four answers in order to release the door.

04.1⚡ Circuit A
Calculate −6 × 4
Answer: -24
Hint

Different signs produce a negative product.

Worked example

−3 × 5 = −15.

Mini-lesson

Multiplication: two numbers with the same sign produce a positive answer; different signs produce a negative answer. Multiply the magnitudes: 6 × 4 = 24.

04.2🔋 Circuit B
Calculate −42 ÷ (−7)
Answer: 6
Hint

Two negative signs make a positive quotient.

Worked example

−24 ÷ (−6) = 4.

Mini-lesson

Division uses the same sign rules as multiplication: same signs → positive; different signs → negative. 42 ÷ 7 = 6.

04.3🌀 Circuit C
Calculate 8 × (−3)
Answer: -24
Hint

Positive multiplied by negative gives a negative answer.

Worked example

7 × (−2) = −14.

Mini-lesson

Multiply the absolute values, then decide the sign. 8 × 3 = 24 and the signs differ, so the result is −24.

04.4🔌 Circuit D
Calculate −56 ÷ 8
Answer: -7
Hint

Different signs give a negative quotient.

Worked example

−30 ÷ 5 = −6.

Mini-lesson

56 ÷ 8 = 7. Since one number is negative and one is positive, the quotient is −7.

ROOM 05 · 4 PUZZLES

The Security Vault

The vault needs four mixed integer calculations. Work carefully with signs, brackets and order of operations to recover the security code.

05.1🗝️ Vault A
Calculate −8 + 3 × 5
Answer: 7
Hint

Multiply before adding.

Worked example

−4 + 2 × 6 = −4 + 12 = 8.

Mini-lesson

Apply BEDMAS: multiplication first, so −8 + 15 = 7.

05.2📦 Vault B
Calculate (−6 − 4) ÷ (−2)
Answer: 5
Hint

Calculate inside brackets first.

Worked example

(−3 − 9) ÷ (−4) = −12 ÷ (−4) = 3.

Mini-lesson

Inside the brackets, −6 − 4 = −10. Dividing two negatives gives a positive result: 5.

05.3🧲 Vault C
Calculate 18 − (−3 × 4)
Answer: 30
Hint

First multiply −3 by 4, then subtract that negative number.

Worked example

10 − (−2 × 3) = 10 − (−6) = 16.

Mini-lesson

−3 × 4 = −12, so 18 − (−12) = 18 + 12 = 30.

05.4🧰 Vault D
Calculate −2 × (7 − 11) + 3
Answer: 11
Hint

Brackets first: 7 − 11 = −4.

Worked example

−3 × (2 − 6) + 1 = −3 × (−4) + 1 = 13.

Mini-lesson

7 − 11 = −4; −2 × (−4) = 8; then 8 + 3 = 11.

ROOM 06 · 6 PUZZLES

The Direction Decoder

Decide whether each phrase represents a positive (+) or negative (−) value or change, using the stated reference directions. Record the six symbols to unlock the navigation door.

06.1📈 Signal A
The temperature increases by 6°C. Is the change + or −?
Answer: +
Hint

An increase is a positive change.

Worked example

An increase of 4°C is +4°C.

Mini-lesson

Positive changes represent increases from a starting value. The sign describes the change, not necessarily the final temperature.

06.2🧭 Signal B
North is positive on this map. A vehicle travels 9 km south. Is its displacement + or −?
Answer: -
Hint

South is opposite to the stated positive direction.

Worked example

If east is positive, travelling west is negative.

Mini-lesson

Directions are only positive or negative after choosing a reference direction. Here north is positive, so south is negative.

06.3🏦 Signal C
A bank account receives a $25 deposit. Is the change + or −?
Answer: +
Hint

Money added to an account is a positive change.

Worked example

A $10 deposit changes the balance by +$10.

Mini-lesson

Deposits increase a bank balance; withdrawals decrease it.

06.4🌊 Signal D
Sea level is 0 m. A submarine is 12 m below sea level. Is its position + or −?
Answer: -
Hint

Below the reference level of zero is negative.

Worked example

3 m below sea level is −3 m.

Mini-lesson

A position below a zero reference level is negative. Above that level is positive.

06.5📉 Signal E
A company profit decreases by $7. Is the change + or −?
Answer: -
Hint

The word decreases describes a negative change.

Worked example

A decrease of 5 is a change of −5.

Mini-lesson

Decreases are negative changes, even if the final value remains above zero.

06.6🛗 Signal F
An elevator rises 4 floors. Is its change in floor number + or −?
Answer: +
Hint

Moving up increases the floor number.

Worked example

Rising 2 floors is a change of +2.

Mini-lesson

An upward movement is a positive change when higher floors have larger numbers.

ROOM 07 · 4 PUZZLES

The Final Escape

The last door is protected by a bracket-placement machine. Click two gaps to insert ONE pair of brackets so that each equation becomes true. All four equations must be correct to escape.

07.1🧩 Bracket Gate A
Insert one pair of brackets into 6 + 2 × 3 to make 24.
Answer: (6 + 2) × 3 = 24
Hint

To get 24, make the addition happen before multiplication.

Worked example

(4 + 2) × 3 = 18, but 4 + (2 × 3) = 10.

Mini-lesson

Brackets change the order of operations. Without brackets, multiplication happens before addition. Put brackets around the first two numbers.

07.2🧪 Bracket Gate B
Insert one pair of brackets into −4 + 10 × 3 to make 18.
Answer: (−4 + 10) × 3 = 18
Hint

Try adding −4 and 10 first.

Worked example

(−2 + 8) × 4 = 24.

Mini-lesson

First calculate −4 + 10 = 6, then multiply 6 × 3 = 18. A negative number may appear inside the brackets.

07.3🛰️ Bracket Gate C
Insert one pair of brackets into 18 ÷ 3 + 3 to make 3.
Answer: 18 ÷ (3 + 3) = 3
Hint

Try making the divisor larger by grouping the last two numbers.

Worked example

24 ÷ (4 + 2) = 4.

Mini-lesson

Brackets around 3 + 3 give 6. Then 18 ÷ 6 = 3. Without brackets, 18 ÷ 3 + 3 = 9.

07.4🚀 Bracket Gate D
Insert one pair of brackets into 20 − 4 × 3 to make 48.
Answer: (20 − 4) × 3 = 48
Hint

Make the subtraction happen before the multiplication.

Worked example

(12 − 5) × 2 = 14.

Mini-lesson

(20 − 4) × 3 = 16 × 3 = 48. Without brackets, 20 − 4 × 3 = 8.
