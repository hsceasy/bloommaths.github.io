Viorosa Maths · Year 11 · Mathematics Standard

Medication dosage formulas

Key idea

These historical formulas are mathematical models only, not safe clinical dosing methods. All quantities below are fictional worksheet values, not medication instructions. A is the adult model quantity and D is the child model quantity, in the same fictional units.

Fried’s formula

D = mA150

m is age in months.

Young’s formula

D = aAa + 12

a is age in years.

Clark’s formula

D = wA150

w is weight in pounds (lb).

Worked example 1 — Fried’s formula

Question: A historical mathematics worksheet models a quantity for an infant aged 1 year and 3 months. The adult reference quantity is 100 fictional units. Calculate the modelled child quantity.

Solution

m = 12 + 3

m = 15 months

D = mA150

D = 15 × 100150

D = 1500150

D = 10 fictional units

Worked example 2 — Young’s formula

Question: A student reads a historical formula table showing an adult reference quantity of 120 fictional units and a child model quantity of 40 units. Determine the age used in the calculation.

Solution

D = aAa + 12

40 = 120aa + 12

40(a + 12) = 120a

40a + 480 = 120a

480 = 120a − 40a

480 = 80a

a = 48080

a = 6 years

Worked example 3 — Clark’s formula

Question: A fictional worksheet uses a weight of 50 lb and a child model quantity of 20 units. Determine the adult reference quantity used in the worksheet.

Solution

D = wA150

20 = 50A150

20 × 150 = 50A

3000 = 50A

A = 300050

A = 60 fictional units

Check units, brackets and each inverse operation before finalising your answer.
