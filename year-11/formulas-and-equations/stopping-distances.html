Stopping distances

Key idea

Stopping distance includes the distance travelled before braking begins and the distance travelled while braking. Use consistent units.

Speed

v = dt

d is distance and t is time.

Distance

d = vt

v is speed and t is time.

Time

t = dv

Divide distance by speed.

Speed conversion

v (m/s) = v (km/h)3.6

Convert before using seconds and metres.

Reaction distance

R = vtᵣ

v is speed in m/s and tᵣ is reaction time in seconds.

Braking distance

B = kv²

k is a supplied model constant, dependent on conditions and units.

Stopping distance

S = R + B

R is reaction distance and B is braking distance, both in metres.

Worked example 1 — stopping distance

Question: During a simulated road safety test, a car travels at 72 km/h. The driver takes 1.5 seconds to react to a signal and the car travels another 28 m while braking. Calculate the total stopping distance.

Solution

v = 72 ÷ 3.6

v = 20 m/s

R = vtᵣ

R = 20 × 1.5

R = 30 m

S = R + B

S = 30 + 28

S = 58 m

Worked example 2 — reaction time

Question: A test vehicle travelling at 16 m/s stops 55 m after a signal appears. Its braking distance is recorded as 31 m. Calculate the time between the signal and the start of braking.

Solution

S = R + B

55 = R + 31

R = 55 − 31

R = 24 m

R = vtᵣ

24 = 16tᵣ

tᵣ = 2416

tᵣ = 1.5 s

Worked example 3 — braking model

Question: An engineer uses the model B = 0.08v², with speed in metres per second. Calculate the modelled braking distance at 90 km/h.

Solution

v = 90 ÷ 3.6

v = 25 m/s

B = 0.08(25)²

B = 0.08 × 625

B = 50 m

Check units, brackets and each inverse operation before finalising your answer.
